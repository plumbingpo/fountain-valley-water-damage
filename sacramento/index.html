Where Water Damage Starts in Sacramento Properties

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. These are the ones Sacramento property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. For roof leaks in Sacramento, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. When the clog becomes complete, every flush and every load of laundry comes back out downstairs. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. Short downspouts, soil that slopes toward the house and blocked footing drains are frequent causes. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. We remove any standing water in Sacramento homes, dry the walls and slab, and explain what likely caused it.

Simple Ways to Keep a Sacramento Home Dry

Most of the water damage we see in Sacramento homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Begin at the hoses. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. If you find something in your Sacramento home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.
